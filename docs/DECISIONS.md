## Phase 3

- T3.1 (2026-11-11): nodes are symbols (same symbol = same sid, so a symbol never maps to two sids and CA/symbol joins can use the symbol alone; `valid_from/valid_to` are informational). Edge B additionally skips a candidate B that already has an incoming official edge (a node may have one in-edge). Official edges are accepted closest-date-first, auto edges by `(last_date, symbol)`; a union-find rejects cycles. "Sessions between" is the difference of 0-based indices in the distinct `bhavcopy_eq` date list (all series). `sid_merges.csv` is appended to across runs; `rename_autodetect.csv` is rewritten each run. If an earlier auto-detected edge disappears later (a second candidate shows up), the two symbols keep their (shared) existing sid; sids are never re-numbered.
- T3.2 (2026-11-11): `compute_gap_factors(rows, ca)` takes `rows` = (sid, date, session_idx, open, close, prev_close) and `ca` with `sid` already joined (an optional `face_fallback` column feeds rights), and returns a SPARSE audit frame (only rows needing a factor, plus `ca_ignored` rows dated at the ex-date; extra columns `ca_types`, `note`); `build_daily_panel` joins it to the full rows (gap_factor 1.0, gap_src '' elsewhere). Every delivery or intraday BUY also leaves a cushion of (DP + minimum brokerage) x (1 + GST) = Rs 29.5 of cash, and a SELL whose costs would overdraw cash is rejected `cost_exceeds_cash`: without these a dust position (value below its own exit fees) drove cash negative, breaking the cash >= -0.01 invariant. An intraday position that cannot trade at the close (locked, no bar) is closed by force at the last close with fees and no slippage, reason `squareoff_forced`. Delisting write-off follows the card (20 sessions without a row and none later in the data -> 80% of the last close, no costs). Reference policies: BuyAndHold, EqualWeightUniverse (open of t+1; trades only names more than `band` off target), and the sleeve policies Clairvoyant / RandomPolicy (one sleeve of 1/h of equity per session: buy at the close of t+1, sell at the open of t+1+h). `labels/forward.py` does not exist on this branch, so `c2o_forward_labels(daily, sessions, h)` in engine.py computes the clairvoyant label from adj_close / adj_open. Truth test (synthetic market, planted_ic=0.10): Clairvoyant (k=5, h=5, Rs 25 lakh) net Sharpe 16.7; RandomPolicy (k=10) mean daily net return -17.5 bps against an expectation of -16.9 bps (1 SE 3.2 bps; the expectation is mean(sleeve fraction) x mean label minus the measured fee+slippage drag). Rs 5 lakh was too small for that test: with k=20 the Rs 5,000 positions pay about 2% round-trip in fixed fees and the book decays to nothing, which is the fixed-fee effect the card wants to see, not a leak. Engine runtime: 0.15 s for the 601-session synthetic market (40 names); 0.4 s for 3,000 sessions x 150 names with 6,718 fills. The `stocksense backtest` CLI and the real-data acceptance run are not part of this commit.

### D11
- `dens` (`models/dens.py`, key `dens`, trial family `g2-dens`) follows Zhang et al. 2020 / Qlib `DEnsembleModel` from memory of the paper, not from reading the reference code (no network use). Choices where the card left room: sub-model 0 uses all features and uniform weights; after sub-model k the loss curve is the squared error at `n_snapshots=6` cumulative tree counts (one incremental prediction pass); h = mean of the pct-rank of the ensemble-so-far squared error and the pct-rank of the loss-rank slope (last third minus first third of the normalised curve); 10 bins by h; weight = 1/(decay^k * mean_h(bin) + 0.1), rescaled to mean 1 and multiplied into the walk-forward sample weights. - 2026-11-20 G2-E1 (volatility forecasts): `features/volforecast.py`. Daily realised vol `rv` = Garman-Klass (adjusted OHLC) over the full history, replaced by the 5-minute `intraday_rv` where a full 75-bar session exists (2024-09 on), floored at 0.2% a day (an unfloored ln RV has a far left tail from degenerate ranges). HAR is the log-RV pooled regression of the card (regressors are ln RV_t, the 5-row mean and the 22-row mean of ln RV), refit every January on rows whose TARGET date is <= the previous 31 December (needs >= 20,000 rows); the forecast is `scale * exp(f + s2/2)` where `s2` is the residual variance (lognormal mean) and `scale` = sqrt(sum la^2 / sum rv^2) on the fit sample, because a range-based RV excludes the overnight gap and the S1 stop and weights are in `vol_21` (close-to-close) units. `har_vol_1d` = the 1-step forecast, `har_vol_10d` = sqrt of the sum of the ten iterated daily variances. GARCH(1,1) (zero mean, normal, `arch` 9.0.0, licence NCSA = permissive; added to pyproject and uv.lock) is fitted per stock at each month end on the last <= 1,000 returns (>= 500 needed) and its 10-step variance sum is held for the next calendar month, as the card says (so a forecast can be up to ~21 sessions stale; daily filtering with frozen parameters would be fresher but is not what the card specifies). Unit tests: HAR coefficients recovered within 0.05 on a simulated HAR process (30 stocks x 2,500 days), GARCH(1,1) (omega 1e-6, alpha .08, beta .9) 10-day vol within 15% of the true conditional vol on 5 seeds, truncation test on the synthetic market (non-vacuous).

- 2026-11-20 G2-E2 (James-Stein decile map): `expected_return_map_decile(..., shrink="james_stein")` (default `"k"` = the C2 fixed-k shrinkage, unchanged) and `G1Config.er_map = "decile_js"` (spec key only when non-default, so every old hash is unchanged). Per date t the window's decile means m_j with sampling variance v_j = s_j^2 / n_j (s_j^2 the within-decile label variance over the window), mu = mean(m_j), tau^2 = max(0, var(m_j) - mean(v_j)), m*_j = mu + (1 - v_j/(v_j + tau^2)) (m_j - mu), then the same isotonic step weighted by n_j. Hand tests: tau^2 = 0 gives mu for every decile; v -> 0 gives m_j. Caveat recorded for the verdict: labels overlap (10-day horizon) and dates are cross-sectionally correlated, so n_j in v_j overstates the information; with ~25,000 rows per decile v_j is tiny and the shrinkage is mild, like the fixed-k rule (k = 200 against n_j ~ 25,000).
- 2026-11-20 G2-E3 (fractional Kelly): `S1Config.sizing in {score_over_vol (default), kelly}` and `kelly_c` (default 0.5). Raw weight of a new entry = `kelly_c * er / sigma_h^2`, `sigma_h = vol * sqrt(h)` with `vol` the selected vol model; names with `er <= 0` get 0; the meta multiplier still applies; the existing caps and `allocate_weights` still apply. Decision on what "gross target" means for Kelly: `allocate_weights` rescales raw weights to sum to the room, which would cancel `c` entirely (c = 0.25 and 0.5 would give identical books). So under Kelly the room is `min(room, sum(raw))`: the book deploys c * er / sigma^2 per name and only reaches the volatility-targeted gross when the Kelly weights exceed it. With er in the 10-100 bp range and 10-day sigma of 5-8%, Kelly weights at c = 0.25-0.5 are large (each name ~0.05-0.3), so in practice both c values saturate the room and may give the same or nearly the same book; the grid reports that as the result.

- 2026-11-20 G2-E5 (OU mean reversion): `features/ou.py`. Residual `e = la - beta_{t-1} lm - industry mean of (la - beta lm)` (beta = 63-row regression slope, shifted one row; the industry term is 0 for UNKNOWN and for industries with < 5 members that day; the mean includes the stock itself). X = cumulative `e` over 60 rows, AR(1) by OLS (df = n - 2 for the residual variance), `kappa = -ln b * 252`, `m = a/(1-b)`, `sigma_eq = sqrt(var/(1-b^2))`; `ou_sscore = (X_t - m)/sigma_eq` only when 0 < b < 1 and half-life ln2/(-ln b) <= 30 sessions, `ou_halflife` whenever 0 < b < 1, `resid_rev_5 = -sum_5 e`. Windows are vectorised (sliding windows per stock; a NaN or a window spanning more than ceil(1.25*60)+2 sessions is null) and a test checks the rolling result equals the scalar fit. Known property recorded for the E9 verdict: a 60-point AR(1) fit under-estimates b by about (1+3b)/n, so single-window kappa is biased high (the test recovers kappa = 20 within 25% on a 6,000-point path; on 60 points it would not); the bias is common to all names so ranks are barely affected, but the absolute half-life should not be read as calibrated. Pairs: each January, on the last 252 sessions up to the previous 31 December, per industry the top-50 by median turnover with a complete window; Engle-Granger (OLS of the lower-index log price on the other, ADF with 0 lags and no constant on the residual, MacKinnon p for N=2 via `statsmodels.tsa.adfvalues.mackinnonp`, tried only when t < -1.5), kept when p < 0.05 and the half-life from b = 1 + gamma is 5 to 30; each stock keeps its lowest-p pair; `pair_z` = spread (fixed alpha, beta) minus its own last-60 mean over its std, sign +1 for the dependent leg and -1 for the other. Only one regression direction is tested (a pair is not tested both ways). The zero-lag ADF is a simplification of statsmodels `coint` (which picks lags automatically) for speed; with a 252-row window the difference in the number of pairs found is small. Test on a simulated AR(1) spread (b 0.93, 500 rows) finds the pair (beta within 0.1 of 1.5, p < 0.05) and not an unrelated random walk; selection does not change when data after the cutoff is overwritten; truncation test of both feature groups (non-vacuous).
